Partnership & Investment Prospectus · Round 01 · Three directions

Not a proposal.
A legacy, in type.

Three ways the book could look and feel — each one a complete system: colorway, typefaces, how a cover, an invitation, a chapter opener, the centerpiece spread and the infographic pages are built. Pick one (or mix) and every remaining page follows its rules. All of them take the inspo folder's cues — oversized type that bleeds off pages, split-color letters, text laid over photography, duotone and halftone images, rotated titles, giant numerals — and tune them to the brief's elegant, timeless, historic, modern.

How to read this. Every page below is drawn as a true-proportion Letter-size spread (17 × 11 in) — change the trim and it all reflows. Imagery is Cynthia's current renderings and portraits, as placeholders. Copy is lifted from the prospectus draft. Numbers shown as 000 are placeholders — the dashboard needs real figures. The one hard number used is 250, the occupancy on the architect's G‑3 sheet.

Direction A · “Monument Type”

Brick & Bone

The building is made of brick; the book is made of type. Condensed capitals are stacked like courses of masonry, cropped by the page edge and carried across the gutter, while a quiet italic serif does the speaking voice. It reads as an exhibition catalogue for a very confident cultural institution — loud headlines, generous calm everywhere else.

Borrowed from the inspo“Anthropocene”, “Free Culture” and “NY” — headline letters too big for the page, split between two colors, bleeding across the spine.
Image treatmentBlack-and-white or brick duotone photography; type laid over with multiply so letters sit inside the picture, as in the NY and Brutalism spreads.
Where the brief livesWarm brick, charcoal, ivory and a hairline of gold from the brief's palette — all pulled from Camp Greene's actual brickwork and the building's red door.

The rules of the book — what every remaining page obeys
  • The brick line. A 0.5 pt brick rule runs the full height of the page at the outer margin of every text page. It is the book's spine of color.
  • Crop once. Every chapter opener lets one display word run off the trim or across the gutter. Never twice on a spread.
  • One red word. A headline gets at most one brick word; a page gets at most one brick object bigger than a label.
  • Photography is B&W or brick duotone inside the book. Full color is saved for the cover, the two night plates, and portraits of people.
  • Type goes on the picture. Display words overprint photos with multiply (dark type) or screen (light type) — never a box behind a headline.
  • Gold is hairline only. 0.5 pt foil rules, one per spread at most. It never fills an area.
  • Grid. 12 columns, 0.75 in margins, 0.125 in bleed. Text lives in 3-column-wide measures (≈ 45 characters) with at least one empty column beside it.
  • White space is a color. At least a third of every text page is untouched bone.

    Direction B · “Arch & Mosaic”

    Camp Greene

    The warmest of the three. A wide, confident grotesk — letters stretched like a porch — meets a soft, old-style serif for the voice. Photographs are cut into arches (the doorway, the window, the portico) and broken up by color-block mosaics, so the community's faces and the building's brickwork feel stitched together like a quilt. Forest green, brass and clay on ivory: heritage, garden, hospitality.

    Borrowed from the inspo“World of Black Film” and “Ao lado de Lina” — wide type crossing a photograph; “Encircling” — color blocks that step over a portrait; “Letter from the Editors” — tracked caps.
    Image treatmentFull-color warm photography cut to arch windows, or forest–ivory duotone. Blocks of flat color step across the picture and carry the captions and quotes.
    Where the brief livesDeep forest green, soft gold and natural wood tones — plus the brick red re-tuned as a warm “clay” accent. The most literal reading of the brief's palette.

    The rules of the book — what every remaining page obeys
    • Everything stands in an arch. Portraits, hero images and plates that are “about people” are cropped to an arch window — flat edge at the bottom, as if standing on the floor of the page.
    • The mosaic module. Color blocks are always one square (⅛ of the page width). They step diagonally across a photograph, never more than five per page, and may carry a caption, a number or nothing.
    • Marker highlights. One key phrase per page is marked in brass (or clay with ivory type) — the hand-highlighted look, never a bold or underline.
    • Wide type touches the photo. Display words must overlap their image by at least a letter-height; they run in ivory or brass over dark pictures.
    • Forest is the night. Section dividers and the contents page go full forest; ivory pages always follow a forest page.
    • Brass hairline = new idea. A 0.5 pt brass rule is used once per page to open a new thought or caption group.
    • Grid. 8 × 8 square module, 0.75 in margins, 0.125 in bleed. Text measure 40–46 characters. Two columns on facing pages, never three.
    • Warm, not corporate. Photographs keep their warmth; nothing is cooled, desaturated or flattened.

      Direction C · “Marquee & Blueprint”

      Night Gallery

      The building after dark, as a book. A razor-sharp didone serif set enormous — roman and italic mixed inside a single headline — glows like the neon sign over the door. Photographs are printed in halftone and red or gold ink; the architect's own drawings (Circa Architecture, G‑3 and AS‑1) are laid over them as blueprint line-work. Information pages borrow the grammar of a drawing sheet: hairline frames, mono annotations, a title block. The most luxurious and the most “information-driven” of the three.

      Borrowed from the inspo“Anthropocene” — monumental type on black with one word in red; “Le Corbusier” and “Laurie Anderson” — halftone photography and rotated titles; the coral serif quote page; “Born to Run” — mono captions.
      Image treatmentHalftone dot screens in black, vermilion or gold ink; full-color night renderings kept dark and cinematic; white-line blueprint overlays from the real construction set.
      Where the brief lives“Architectural prospectus” and “luxury real-estate publication” read most literally here — historic charcoal, warm white, soft gold, with the red door's vermilion as the signal color.

      The rules of the book — what every remaining page obeys
      • Dark opens, light reads. Covers, dividers, chapter plates and the infographic pages run on ink; running text always sits on warm white.
      • One glowing word. Each dark spread may light a single word in gold (or vermilion for “you are here”). No other element glows.
      • Roman + italic in one line. Every Bodoni headline switches to italic at least once. The italic word carries the meaning.
      • Blueprint under picture. Every chapter plate has a white-line drawing — floor plan, site plan or elevation — blended under or over the photograph, as the Roadmap section's signature.
      • Halftone for people, full color for the building. Portraits and community photographs are printed in dot screen (ink, vermilion or gold); the night renderings stay in full color.
      • Every fact gets a mono caption. Figure number + address or drawing reference (“FIG. 06 — A-2 FRONT ELEVATION”). Real references to the architect's sheets add credibility.
      • The sheet frame. Data pages carry a 0.5 pt gold border and a title block (project · address · drawings by · issue date) in the lower right.
      • Grid. 6-column drawing grid, 0.75 in margins, 0.125 in bleed. Text measure 45 characters; captions hang in the outer margin.
